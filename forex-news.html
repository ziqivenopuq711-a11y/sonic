Analysis — May 10, 2026

Dollar Dominance Under Pressure as Tokenized Treasuries Expand Cross-Border Settlement Alternatives

The U.S. dollar's role as the world's primary reserve and settlement currency is being tested on two fronts simultaneously: geopolitical fragmentation encouraging regional currency blocs, and the emergence of tokenized dollar-denominated assets that allow cross-border capital movement without traditional correspondent banking rails.

Tokenized U.S. Treasuries — led by products from Ondo Finance, Franklin Templeton, and BlackRock — are enabling sovereign wealth funds and institutional investors in Asia, the Middle East, and Latin America to access dollar yield without routing transactions through the SWIFT system. This development is both a reinforcement of dollar demand (in the form of T-bill purchases) and a structural disruption to the correspondent banking infrastructure that has historically made dollar transactions expensive and slow for non-U.S. entities.

  • Over $3.2 billion in tokenized U.S. Treasuries now held by non-U.S. entities via on-chain platforms
  • DeFi lending protocols offer dollar-denominated yields accessible from any blockchain wallet globally
  • Central banks in Singapore, UAE, and Brazil piloting CBDC interoperability frameworks with tokenized assets
  • IMF warns of potential regulatory arbitrage as on-chain dollar exposure bypasses AML screening

For forex traders, these dynamics are creating new correlations worth monitoring. The DXY index's relationship with crypto market sentiment has strengthened over the past 18 months as stablecoin flows have grown large enough to influence short-term dollar liquidity. A sustained risk-off event in crypto markets now demonstrably tightens dollar funding conditions in certain emerging market currencies.
