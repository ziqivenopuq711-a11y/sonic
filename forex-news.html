Dollar Weakens as Fed Signals Prolonged Rate Pause; Crypto Markets Rally

The US Dollar Index (DXY) has retreated to a two-year low following Federal Reserve Chairman Jerome Powell's testimony before the Senate Banking Committee, where he emphasized that the central bank sees no urgency to adjust rates in either direction given persistently subdued inflation and a resilient but cooling labor market. The dollar's weakness has historically acted as a tailwind for risk assets including cryptocurrencies, and this correlation has played out precisely, with Bitcoin gaining 8% in the week following the testimony.

Currency strategists at major investment banks have revised their DXY forecasts lower, with the consensus now targeting a range of 98-102 through year-end, compared to the current reading of 100.4. The euro has been a primary beneficiary, strengthening toward 1.14 against the dollar as the European Central Bank maintains a relatively hawkish tone despite slowing Eurozone growth. Emerging market currencies have also posted gains, reducing pressure on economies with dollar-denominated debt obligations.

Stablecoins

USDT and USDC Combined Market Cap Surpasses $250 Billion, Rivaling Major Central Bank Reserve Holdings

The aggregate market capitalization of USD-pegged stablecoins Tether (USDT) and USD Coin (USDC) has crossed $250 billion for the first time, a milestone that positions the stablecoin duopoly as a significant force in global dollar liquidity alongside traditional monetary instruments. Cross-border payment companies and emerging market businesses increasingly use stablecoins to settle international invoices, bypassing correspondent banking networks that impose high fees and multi-day settlement delays. This demand has proven resilient even during periods of crypto market volatility, confirming stablecoins' transition from speculative instruments to genuine financial infrastructure.

Central Banks

Bank of Japan Ends Decades-Long Ultra-Low Rate Policy, Yen Strengthens Against All Major Currencies

The Bank of Japan has raised its benchmark interest rate to 1.25% — the highest level in over 25 years — following persistent wage growth that has finally convinced policymakers that Japan's deflationary cycle has decisively ended. The yen responded by strengthening sharply against the US dollar and euro, with USD/JPY falling from 155 to below 149 over the course of a week. For crypto traders, the shift matters because the carry trade unwinding that typically accompanies yen strengthening historically drives volatility across risk assets. Market participants are monitoring whether the BOJ's tightening trajectory will pressure global liquidity conditions.

Digital Currencies

European Central Bank Advances Digital Euro to Pilot Stage With 5,000 Merchants Participating

The European Central Bank has advanced the digital euro from research phase to a structured pilot program involving 5,000 merchants and approximately 50,000 consumers across Germany, France, Spain, and Italy. The pilot tests programmable payment features, offline transaction capability, and privacy-preserving mechanisms that distinguish the digital euro from commercial bank payment systems. Financial analysts are closely watching for signals about the digital euro's eventual design, which could significantly influence the competitive landscape for private stablecoins operating in the Eurozone.
